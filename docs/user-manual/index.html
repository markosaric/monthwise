1. Getting started

Use the menu on the left to move between screens. The button next to the logo collapses the menu to icons. Set the app up once, in this order:

  1. Settings → Cards and accounts: add your bank accounts and payment cards. Mark your main current account as Primary and give it a starting balance so balances match your bank.
  2. Settings → Recurring bills: add the bills that arrive every month (electricity, internet, building reserve…).
  3. Loans: add your loans and leases once; their monthly payments then appear on their own.
  4. Month → Add input: add your salary as a Recurring input so it shows up every month.

Amounts are in euros. Anything Monthwise can calculate (installments, loan payments, card payments, contributions) is calculated for you and only needs to be ticked off.

2. Dashboard

The home screen answers “how is this month going?”.

  • Quick add buttons under the title: add an input, bill, expense, cash withdrawal or card purchase.
  • Four tiles: inputs (and how much is received), outputs (and how much is paid), the month result, and what is still to pay.
  • Red banner: appears only when something is overdue. Open the Month screen to pay it.
  • Coming up: payments and inputs due in the next 7 days.
  • Charts: inputs vs. outputs of the last six months and this month's outputs by kind. The tiles at the bottom link to loans, cards, shared costs and recurring bills.

3. Month

The working screen: everything that comes in and goes out in one month. Use the arrows next to the title to move between months.

  • Tick the circle in front of a row to mark it Received or Paid. Overdue rows are marked in red.
  • Income: Add input for salary and other inputs. Contributions from people who share costs with you are grouped under their name.
  • Bills: Add recurring bills creates this month's bills from your recurring list; open a bill to enter the amount and dates once it arrives. Add bill adds a one-off bill.
  • Expenses: one-off spending such as groceries. Cash withdrawal records cash taken from an account.
  • Loan payments and Card payments are filled in automatically from your loans and card purchases.
  • Click a row's arrow to see details and to edit or delete it.

A bill or expense paid with a credit or charge card is not taken from your account straight away: it becomes part of that card's next card payment.

4. Cash flow

Shows inputs and outputs over time: This month (day by day), Last 12 months or This year. Use Filters to narrow by payer, account or category, and Show as table for the numbers. Click a month in the list for its monthly report.

5. Cards and installments

The Cards screen shows the balance of each account now and at the end of the month, and every card that allows installments.

  • Add purchase: choose the card, amount and date. Turn on In monthly installments and set the number of installments; Monthwise spreads the amount over the card payments.
  • Open a card to see its upcoming card payments month by month and its purchases with what remains to be charged.
  • A card payment is ticked off as paid on the Month screen or on the card itself.

6. Loans and leasing

All bank loans and leases with monthly payment, progress and what is left to repay. Add loan asks for the lender, start month, payment day, monthly amount and number of payments (and a residual value for a lease).

Open a loan to see each payment. Add payment change records a new monthly amount or payment day from a given month (e.g. after a rate change). Close loan ends a loan that is paid off early.

Payments by standing order are marked paid automatically on their date. Payments by bill wait until you tick them.

7. Shared costs

For costs you split with someone (a partner or flatmate). Add the person under Add participant, then set their share on a recurring bill or loan. Each month Monthwise calculates what they owe you and lists it here and as an expected input on the Month screen. Tick a contribution when you receive the money.

8. Settings

Lists the app uses everywhere: cards and accounts, payers, input categories, expense categories, loan categories and recurring bills. Add your own entries next to the starting ones. A category that is already in use cannot be deleted; hide it instead.

Card numbers and IBANs are stored encrypted and shown only on request.

The Data and backups tile, in the Data group, exports and imports all your data; see Data and backups.

9. On the phone

Monthwise works in a phone browser too. The menu moves to a bar at the bottom of the screen; everything else works the same.

10. Installing Monthwise

Monthwise runs in one of three ways. All three use the same version of the app, and its data can move between them.

WayBest forCost
Docker one-clickStarting out on your Mac or Windows PC; the same package later runs on an always-on machine€0
Native background serviceYour Mac or Windows PC without Docker; starts by itself when you log in€0
AzureReaching Monthwise from anywhere over HTTPS, once login existsabout €15–20 per month after the free first year

To move Monthwise from one way to another, export your data on the old one and import it on the new one (see Data and backups).

Every way needs the GitHub CLI, because the Monthwise repository is private. Sign in once with gh auth login, then allow it to download the app image with gh auth refresh --scopes read:packages.

Your secrets. On the first start Monthwise creates a database password and two encryption keys for card numbers and IBANs, and shows the folder they are in. Copy the files in that folder into your password manager straight away. Without them the stored card numbers and IBANs can never be read again, for example on a new computer. Monthwise never creates new keys for a database that already exists.

Docker one-click

  1. Install Docker Desktop (Mac or Windows) or OrbStack (Mac, lighter). Both are free for personal use.
  2. Download the package: gh release download --repo markosaric/monthwise --pattern "monthwise-docker-*.zip", and unzip it into a folder you keep, for example Documents/Monthwise.
  3. Mac: double-click Monthwise.command. The first time, macOS may ask you to allow it: right-click it, choose Open and confirm. Drag it to the Dock to keep it there.
    Windows: double-click Monthwise.cmd, and pin it to the taskbar or the Start menu.
  4. Monthwise starts, waits until it is ready and opens http://localhost:8080. In Safari, File → Add to Dock (in Edge or Chrome, Install app) gives it its own icon and window.

After the first start, Monthwise comes back by itself whenever Docker starts. Other commands, run in the package folder (./monthwise.sh on a Mac, .\monthwise.ps1 in PowerShell on Windows):

CommandWhat it does
stopStops Monthwise. The data is kept.
update (or update 1.2.0)Downloads the newest (or the given) version and restarts with all data kept.
status / logsShows whether it runs and its version, or the last log lines.
network on / network offAllows or stops connections from other devices, for example your phone through Tailscale. Off by default.
remove (remove --data on a Mac, remove -Data on Windows)Removes Monthwise from Docker and keeps the data, unless you ask for the data to go too and type DELETE.

Where things are. The data is in the Docker volume monthwise-pgdata. The secrets and settings are in ~/Library/Application Support/Monthwise/docker on a Mac and in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Monthwise\docker on Windows. Only your user account can open that folder.

Native background service

Monthwise and its own PostgreSQL database run in the background without Docker and start when you log in. The database listens on port 5442, so it never clashes with a development database.

  1. Mac: install Homebrew, then gh release download --repo markosaric/monthwise --pattern monthwise-native-macos.sh and run bash monthwise-native-macos.sh install. It installs PostgreSQL 17 with Homebrew.
  2. Windows: gh release download --repo markosaric/monthwise --pattern monthwise-native-windows.ps1, then in PowerShell run powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\monthwise-native-windows.ps1 install. PostgreSQL comes with the release; no administrator rights are needed.
  3. Open http://localhost:8080 and add it to the Dock or the taskbar as above.

The installer copies its script into the Monthwise folder: ~/Library/Application Support/Monthwise/native/monthwise.sh on a Mac, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Monthwise\native\monthwise.ps1 on Windows. It takes start, stop, update, status, logs and network on|off like the Docker commands, and uninstall. If a new version does not start, update goes back to the previous one by itself. uninstall keeps the data, secrets and logs unless you add --data (Mac) or -Data (Windows) and type DELETE.

Where things are, in that same native folder: pgdata (the database), config/secrets (the secrets), config/settings.json (the settings) and logs (one log file per day, the last 14 days kept).

Azure

Azure gives Monthwise a public HTTPS address. Use it only once login exists.

One script sets up everything in an EU region (default West Europe) in the resource group rg-monthwise:

ResourceWhat it is forExpected cost per month
Container App (scales to zero)Runs Monthwise. When unused it stops, so the first page after a quiet spell takes a few seconds.€0 within the monthly free grant
PostgreSQL Flexible Server, Burstable B1msThe database, with 7 days of automatic backups€0 for 12 months on a new account, then about €13–15 plus storage
Key VaultThe database password and the encryption keysa few cents
Log AnalyticsThe log, kept 30 days€0 at household use
Budget alertEmails you at 80 % of the budget (default 25) and when the month is forecast to go over it€0

Azure pay-as-you-go has no hard spending cap; the budget alert is created with the first deployment so you are warned in time.

  1. Create a GitHub token with only the read:packages scope (GitHub → Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens), so Azure can download the app image.
  2. In Azure Cloud Shell (Bash), or anywhere with the Azure CLI signed in, get the deploy/azure folder of the repository and run:
    GHCR_USERNAME=<your GitHub user> GHCR_TOKEN=<the token> BUDGET_EMAIL=<your email> ./deploy.sh 1.2.0
  3. The script prints the address of Monthwise and, the first time, how to copy the encryption keys from Key Vault into your password manager.

To update, run the same command with the new version. The data and the secrets are kept. To see the log: az containerapp logs show -g rg-monthwise -n ca-monthwise.

11. Data and backups

Settings → Data and backups saves all your data to one file on your device and brings it back from such a file. The top of the section shows when you last exported and imported.

Export

  1. Choose Export data and enter a password for the file twice (at least 6 characters).
  2. The file downloads, named for example monthwise-export-2026-10-31-0815.monthwise.
Keep the file and its password safe. The file holds all your financial data, including the full card numbers and IBANs, and nothing in it can be read without the password. Monthwise does not keep the password: put it in your password manager. A file whose password is lost cannot be imported by anyone.

Import

  1. Choose Import data, pick an export file and enter its password, then Check file. Nothing changes yet.
  2. Monthwise shows when the file was made and how many cards, loans, inputs, card purchases, bills and expenses it holds, next to what is in Monthwise now.
  3. Type REPLACE and choose Replace all data. An import always replaces all data; it never adds the file to what is there.

Right before an import Monthwise keeps a safety copy of the current data. If the import was a mistake, choose Bring back under Safety copy. If an import fails, nothing is changed. Files from older versions of Monthwise can be imported; a file from a newer version asks you to update Monthwise first. While an import runs, other screens wait a moment.

Reminder and history

The Dashboard reminds you to export when you have not done so for 30 days. Under Export reminder you can turn it off or choose 7, 14, 30, 60 or 90 days. Dismiss hides it until the next interval passes without an export.

The History lists every export, import and safety copy with its time, file and result, including failed attempts. An import does not change the history.

Moving Monthwise

To move to another computer or from Docker to the native service or Azure: export on the old installation, install the new one, and import the file there. The file works even though the new installation has its own encryption keys.

Azure's own backups

On Azure the database also keeps 7 days of automatic backups. In the Azure portal, open the PostgreSQL server in rg-monthwise and choose Restore to create a copy of the database as it was at an earlier moment, then point Monthwise to it. Use this as a second safety net; your own export files stay the main backup.
